East-Facing Balcony Plants

An east-facing balcony gets direct sun from sunrise until late morning, then sits in shade through the hottest part of the day. That makes it one of the easiest balconies for fuchsias, begonias, hydrangeas and leafy crops that scorch or bolt in afternoon heat. How many of those morning hours actually reach your plants depends mostly on the balcony above you and on how deep your balcony is.

Why morning sun is the gentle kind

Morning light arrives while the air is at its coolest and the pots still hold the night's moisture. Leaves warm up slowly, dew dries off early, which helps against fungal leaf spots, and by the time the air temperature peaks in mid-afternoon the balcony is already in shade.

The same hours on a west-facing balcony feel very different, because there the sun lines up with the heat of the day.

The trade-off is intensity. Plants that need six or more hours of strong sun to flower or fruit often grow leggy here unless they stand right at the railing.

How many hours an east balcony really gets

In theory, a wall facing due east sees the sun from sunrise until solar noon, the moment the sun stands due south. On daylight saving time, solar noon usually falls somewhere around 1 p.m. on the clock, earlier or later depending on where you are within your time zone. So on paper, a summer morning can mean direct sun from about 5 or 6 a.m. until close to noon.

In practice, three things cut that down:

  • The balcony above. During the morning the sun climbs and swings south until it disappears behind the slab edge above you. The back of the balcony goes into shade first, the railing last.
  • Side walls. In summer the sun rises north of due east, so a side wall or privacy screen on the north side of your balcony shades it at dawn. In winter the sun rises south of east, and the south-side wall matters instead.
  • What is across the street. Early-morning sun is low and easily blocked by a building or tall trees to the east.

So two east-facing balconies in the same city can differ a lot: a shallow, open one may give the railing five or six hours of sun in June, a deep recess under a slab two or three. Railing, middle and back wall: three different gardens

Treat an east balcony as three zones. The railing gets the longest sun. The middle loses it sooner. On a deep balcony the back wall often gets only the first hour or two after sunrise, then sits in bright shade.

Depth matters more than most people expect. The sun reaches a spot on the floor only while it is lower in the sky than the line from that spot to the edge of the slab above, measured straight out from the wall. For a pot at the back wall of a balcony 6 ft (1.8 m) deep with a ceiling 8 ft (2.4 m) high, that line sits at about 53 degrees. When the sun is off to one side, as it is on an east balcony by late morning, it has to be lower still. That is why the late-morning sun, which is both high and far to the south, almost never reaches the back of a deep east balcony.

Check this on your balcony: on a clear morning, stand a ruler upright at the back wall, in the middle and at the railing. Note the time each one falls into shade, or take a photo every hour from sunrise to noon. Then sort each spot into the site's three classes: full sun is 6 hours or more of direct sun, part sun 3 to 6 hours, shade less than 3 hours.

A solid railing shades the floor behind it when the sun is low, which on an east balcony is most of the morning. Planters hung on the railing often get more sun than anything standing on the floor. See railing planters for sizes and brackets.

Plants that suit an east balcony

These plants like bright mornings and resent hot afternoons. The "best spot" column assumes a typical balcony with a slab above; if yours is open to the sky, everything gets more light and you can move shade lovers further forward.

A simple decision rule

Use the midsummer hours at the railing, from your own check or the simulator, to decide how to plant:

  1. 6 hours or more at the railing: put sun lovers in rail boxes and the plants from the table behind them, with impatiens or astilbe at the back wall.
  2. 3 to 6 hours at the railing: part-sun plants at the railing, fuchsias and begonias in the middle, foliage at the back.
  3. Less than 3 hours anywhere: plant as if the balcony faced north. The north-facing guide has plants for that.

In the Southern Hemisphere an east balcony still gets the morning sun, but the sun arcs through the north. In summer it rises south of east, so the side wall on the south side is the one that steals your dawn light.

FAQ

Is an east-facing balcony full sun or part shade?

Usually part sun at the railing and shade at the back wall. A shallow balcony with no slab above can reach full sun, 6 hours or more, at the railing in summer. In winter, expect much less.

Why do my plants lean toward the railing?

They grow toward the brightest light, which under a slab is always the open side. Turning pots a half turn every week or two keeps them more even.

Do plants on an east balcony need less water?

Usually less than on a west or south balcony, because they are shaded during the hottest hours. Small pots, terracotta and wind can still dry them fast, so water in the morning and check with your finger first.
