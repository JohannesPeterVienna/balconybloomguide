Screening & wind

Balcony Privacy Plants for Containers

The best balcony privacy screen is usually a row of long troughs with either evergreen shrubs, clumping bamboo or a climber on a trellis, chosen by how much sun the spot gets and whether you need privacy in winter too. Whatever you choose, a planted screen taller than the railing catches wind like a sail and adds a lot of weight in one line, so plan the fixing and the load before you plant.

Evergreen or seasonal: decide what you are screening

Start with one question: do you need privacy all year, or mainly when you sit outside from late spring to early fall? Evergreen shrubs and bamboo screen all year but grow slowly and cost more in pot size and weight. Deciduous climbers and annual vines are lighter, faster and cheaper to start, but leave you bare in winter. Grasses are in between: most stay standing as tall, straw-colored stems through winter and are cut back in early spring.

A practical mix for many balconies: one or two evergreens where a neighbor's window looks straight in, and lighter seasonal screening along the rest of the railing.

Container heights depend on pot size, feeding and exposure. On a windy upper floor, expect the lower end of each range, and expect evergreens to show wind burn on the exposed side in a cold, dry winter. In UK terms, bamboo, arborvitae, yew and feather reed grass are generally fully hardy in the ground (RHS H5 to H7), but in a container the roots are always more exposed than the top of the plant.

Evergreen shrubs and bamboo in containers

Arborvitae and yew make dense, narrow screens and tolerate clipping, so you can keep them at railing height plus a foot or two. Yew is one of the few evergreens that copes with real shade, which makes it useful on north-facing balconies. Almost all parts of yew are highly toxic to people, cats, dogs and other animals; do not grow it where children or pets chew plants.

Bamboo needs one strict rule: only clumping types, mainly Fargesia. Running bamboos (for example Phyllostachys) send strong rhizomes that can split plastic pots and escape through drainage holes. Even clumping bamboo fills a container with dense roots within a few years and pushes hard against the sides, so use a strong, straight-sided or slightly flared container (not one narrower at the top, or you will never get the root ball out) and plan to divide it every few years. Fargesia leaves curl in drought and scorch in hot afternoon sun, which makes it a better choice for east- or north-facing balconies than for a hot south-facing one.

Climbers on a trellis in a trough

A climber in a trough with its own freestanding trellis gives the most screen for the least weight and floor space. Use a trough at least 12 in (30 cm) deep and wide, with a trellis that is bolted to the trough or set deep inside it, so the wet mix anchors the frame without drilling into the wall.

  • Star jasmine is evergreen and fragrant where winters are mild; in colder regions it needs a sheltered spot or winter protection.
  • Clematis wants sun on its leaves and cool roots. Shade the trough with a low plant in front or a board.
  • Annual climbers such as runner beans or sweet peas (Lathyrus odoratus) cover a trellis in one season and suit renters who move often. Sweet pea seeds are toxic; keep them away from children and pets.
  • English ivy (Hedera helix) is evergreen and shade tolerant but toxic to pets and invasive in parts of North America, including the Pacific Northwest. Keep it trimmed and in its own container if you use it at all.

Grasses for a softer screen

Tall grasses give a light, moving screen and recover well from wind because they bend instead of resisting it. Feather reed grass stays upright and narrow. Maiden grass (Miscanthus sinensis) grows taller and wider but self-seeds in parts of the eastern and southern US, where it is considered invasive; check local guidance before choosing it. Grasses in pots need full sun and a large container, at least 14 in (35 cm) across, to reach full height.

Wind load and weight of tall screens

A tall planted screen behaves differently from a few pots of flowers. Its dense foliage catches wind like a sail, and the force acts high up, far above the pot, so it works as a lever trying to tip the container over the railing side or across the floor. Wind is stronger on upper floors and at building corners. Run the Wind Exposure Check for your balcony, and read our guide to windy balcony plants and pots before committing to anything taller than the railing.

Three things reduce the risk: a wide, heavy trough rather than a narrow tall pot; fixing the trough or trellis to the railing with straps or brackets, if your building permits it; and keeping the plant clipped so the screen is not taller than you need. Placing troughs on the inside of the railing, never on top of it, is a baseline.

Weight is the price of stability. As a worked example, a trough 36 in long, 12 in wide and 12 in deep (91 × 30 × 30 cm) holds about 22 gallons (85 L) of mix. Wet, that is roughly 110 to 170 lb (50 to 75 kg) before the trough and plant are added, depending on the mix. Three such troughs along a railing put around 350 to 500 lb (160 to 230 kg) in a line at the balcony's edge. Use the Pot Weight Planner to calculate your own setup and ask your building manager or a structural engineer whether it is acceptable; read how much weight a balcony can hold for the background.

Check your lease, HOA or strata rules before installing a screen. Many buildings limit anything above railing height, anything fixed to the railing or facade, or plants that change the look of the building. A screen or pot that falls from a balcony can kill someone below.

FAQ

What is the fastest privacy screen for a balcony?

Annual climbers such as runner beans on a trellis in a trough. Sown or planted in late spring, they can cover a 6 ft (1.8 m) trellis by midsummer in full sun.

Is bamboo safe in a balcony container?

Only clumping bamboo such as Fargesia, in a strong container, and even then it needs dividing every few years. Avoid running bamboos.

Which privacy plants work in shade?

Yew, clumping bamboo in part shade, and ivy. Most grasses and annual climbers need full sun.

Do I need to fix a privacy screen to the railing?

On exposed or upper floors, a tall screen should be secured. Check first whether your building allows fixings on the railing.
