About & method

About Balcony Bloom Guide

Balcony Bloom Guide is a gardening site for people who grow on balconies – usually in rented apartments, often several floors up, with a slab above and a building across the street. It answers questions with your balcony's numbers instead of general labels.

Who publishes the site

The site is published by AdStern, an advertising agency in Vienna, Austria (see the imprint). It is financed by advertising on guide and tool pages. No manufacturer or retailer pays for mentions, and the guides contain no affiliate links.

How the Sun & Shade Simulator calculates

The sun's position is computed with the solar position equations published by the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA solar calculation details) for the 15th of each month, in two-minute steps. The sun counts only when it is more than 2° above the horizon. For each step, a straight line is drawn from three spots on your balcony – on the railing, in the middle and near the back wall – towards the sun. The line is checked against the planes of the balcony: the facade, the slab above, the side walls, a solid railing and, if you enter one, a building opposite modelled as a long wall. If nothing blocks the line, the spot is in direct sun.

The result is clear-sky geometry. It does not know about clouds, trees, awnings, plants on the railing, or reflections from light walls. On a cloudy coast the real light is lower; next to a white wall a shaded spot can be brighter than the hours suggest. The automated tests check the sun position against known values (for example the noon sun height at the June solstice) and the shading against hand-calculated cases.

Weights, wind and water

  • Pot weights use the volume of the pot (minus 1 in or 2.5 cm of headspace) and typical wet weights of potting mixes after watering and draining – from about 0.6 kg per litre for a light peat-perlite mix to about 1.65 kg per litre for garden soil. Real mixes differ; weigh a filled bucket to calibrate. The site never says whether a balcony can carry a load.
  • Wind exposure is a transparent points score from six questions (floor, corner position, surroundings, recess, railing, prevailing wind). It is a rule of thumb, not a measurement.
  • Watering estimates water use from reference evaporation for the weather you choose, the sun hours, a wind factor and the leaf area of the plant, and compares it with the water a pot of that size can hold for the plant.

Sources

Horticultural advice follows established public sources such as the Royal Horticultural Society (rhs.org.uk), including its guidance on balconies and roof gardens, U.S. university extension services such as University of Minnesota Extension and Penn State Extension, the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map and the ASPCA list of toxic and non-toxic plants. Each guide lists what it relies on.

How the content is checked

Every guide was written for this site and checked in a separate fact-check pass before publication: plant hardiness and toxicity against the sources named on the page, every worked example recalculated with the same code the tools use, and every number compared across pages so that the site does not contradict itself. Each page shows the date of its last review; when a page is re-checked or corrected, that date changes.

What the site cannot do

It does not replace a structural engineer, your lease or your building's rules. It cannot see your balcony: if a result contradicts what you observe – a spot that the simulator calls sunny but a tree shades every afternoon – trust your observation and enter the obstacle where you can.

Your data

Everything you enter in the tools stays in your browser (localStorage). There is no account and nothing is uploaded. Details on advertising and analytics are in the privacy policy.

History
