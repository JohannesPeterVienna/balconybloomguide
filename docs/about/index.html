About & method

About Balcony Bloom Guide

Balcony Bloom Guide is a gardening site for people who grow on balconies – usually in rented apartments, often several floors up, with a slab above and a building across the street. It answers questions with your balcony's numbers instead of general labels.

Who publishes the site

The site is published by AdStern, an advertising agency in Vienna, Austria (see the imprint). It is financed by advertising, which can appear on guide, plant profile and tool pages as well as on the home page, the overview pages and this page; the imprint, privacy policy and contact page show none. No manufacturer or retailer pays for mentions, and the guides contain no affiliate links.

How the Sun & Shade Simulator calculates

The sun's position is computed with the solar position equations published by the U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA solar calculation details) for the 15th of each month, in two-minute steps. The sun counts only when it is more than 2° above the horizon. For each step, a straight line is drawn from three spots on your balcony – on the railing, in the middle and near the back wall – towards the sun. The line is checked against the planes of the balcony: the facade, the slab above, the side walls, a solid railing and, if you enter one, a building opposite modelled as a long wall. If nothing blocks the line, the spot is in direct sun.

The result is clear-sky geometry. It does not know about clouds, trees, awnings, plants on the railing, or reflections from light walls. On a cloudy coast the real light is lower; next to a white wall a shaded spot can be brighter than the hours suggest. The automated tests check the sun position against known values (for example the noon sun height at the June solstice) and the shading against hand-calculated cases.

Weights, wind and water

  • Pot weights use the volume of the pot (minus 1 in or 2.5 cm of headspace) and typical wet weights of potting mixes after watering and draining – from about 0.6 kg per litre for a light peat-perlite mix to about 1.65 kg per litre for garden soil. Real mixes differ; weigh a filled bucket to calibrate. The site never says whether a balcony can carry a load.
  • Wind exposure is a transparent points score from six questions (floor, corner position, surroundings, recess, railing, prevailing wind). It is a rule of thumb, not a measurement.
  • Watering estimates water use from reference evaporation for the weather you choose, the sun hours, a wind factor and the leaf area of the plant, and compares it with the water a pot of that size can hold for the plant.
  • Floor load divides the weight of your pot list by the floor area you enter and, if you supply a live-load figure, shows the share the pots alone use, without rating it. It never turns that into a statement about safety.
  • Frost dates and sowing take freeze dates from NOAA's 1991–2020 climate normals for 53 U.S. stations (or the dates you enter) and add the planting offsets of a Colorado State University Extension guide; container sizes come from University of Maryland Extension.

Each tool shows its steps next to the result, and the tables on the guide pages that are marked "computed for this page" are produced by the same code when the site is built, so a table and a tool cannot disagree.

Sources

Horticultural advice follows established public sources such as the Royal Horticultural Society (rhs.org.uk), including its guidance on balconies and roof gardens, U.S. university extension services such as University of Minnesota Extension and Penn State Extension, the USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map and the ASPCA list of toxic and non-toxic plants. Each guide lists what it relies on. Data tables name the publication, its date and the day we read it: NOAA's U.S. Climate Normals for freeze dates, Colorado State University Extension for frost classes and University of Maryland Extension for container sizes.

Not every older guide meets that standard yet: several still name a source only by its organization rather than by the exact page. Where that is the case the source list links to the organization's home page instead of a specific publication.

How the site is made

The texts, tables and calculators were written and programmed for this site with the help of AI language models, on behalf of and under the direction of the publisher. There is no named author, no editorial team of horticulturists and no test garden behind the pages, and the site does not claim any: nothing here reports our own growing trials or measurements. What a page can offer instead is a calculation you can follow step by step and figures traced to the publication they come from.

How the content is checked

Every guide was written for this site and checked in a separate pass before publication: plant hardiness and toxicity against the sources named on the page, every worked example recalculated with the same code the tools use. Some figures that appear on several pages are kept in one data file that all of those pages print from: this is the case for the freeze dates, the crop table and the overwintering zones of lavender and rosemary, and the build stops if one of those pages does not take its zones from the file. Other numbers in the older guides are still typed into each page, so a contradiction between two of them remains possible – please report one if you find it. The calculations are covered by automated tests that run before each release – sun position against known solstice values, pot volumes against closed formulas, the frost-date arithmetic against hand-counted cases – and a browser test opens every page at phone and desktop width. Each page shows the date of its last change.

Corrections

If a number looks wrong, write to the address on the contact page and name the page and the figure. Confirmed errors are corrected in the source data, so a fix reaches every page and tool that uses the figure, and the date at the bottom of the page changes.

What the site cannot do

It does not replace a structural engineer, your lease or your building's rules. It cannot see your balcony: if a result contradicts what you observe – a spot that the simulator calls sunny but a tree shades every afternoon – trust your observation and enter the obstacle where you can.

Your data

Everything you enter in the tools stays in your browser (localStorage). There is no account and nothing is uploaded. A link made with "Copy link" carries the values of that one calculation in the address itself, so whoever receives the link can read them. Details on this, on advertising and on analytics are in the privacy policy.

History

The site started in 2026 as a single page with a three-pot herb plan. It was rebuilt from scratch in October 2026 with the tools and guides you see now; the herb plan lives on, in a longer form, in the guide Balcony herb garden in part shade. In November 2026 the tools gained calculation steps, examples, shareable links and CSV export; the Frost Date & Sowing Planner and the crop and plant reference were added; and the general month-by-month calendar guide was withdrawn in favour of the planner, which counts from real frost dates.
