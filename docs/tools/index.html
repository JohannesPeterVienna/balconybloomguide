Tools

Balcony tools

Four calculators that work in your browser, without an account. They share one saved balcony profile, so the sun hours you calculate once show up on the guide pages and in the watering planner.

Tool

Sun & Shade Simulator for Balconies

Direct sun hours at the railing, middle and back wall of your balcony for every month, from location, facing, depth, ceiling, side walls and buildings opposite.

Tool

How Heavy Is a Pot of Wet Soil? Pot Weight Planner

Estimate the wet weight of balcony pots and troughs from shape, size, potting mix and material, add up all pots and print a data sheet for your landlord.

Tool

Balcony Wind Exposure Check

Score your balcony's wind exposure from floor level, position, surroundings, recess, railing and prevailing wind, with pot, fixing and plant advice per class.

Tool

Balcony Watering & Holiday Planner

Estimate how fast each balcony pot dries from its size, material, sun hours and wind, how often to check it, and how big a reservoir you need for days away.

In which order to use them

Start with the sun: the simulator tells you which spot on your balcony counts as full sun, part sun or shade in each month, and that decides what you can grow where. Then plan the pots: once you know which plants go where, the pot weight planner adds up what the filled containers weigh, so you can keep heavy pots near the wall and hand a data sheet to your building manager if you plan something big.

The wind check matters most above the third or fourth floor and on corners; its result also feeds the watering planner, because wind dries pots faster. The watering planner comes last, when you know the pots, their sun and the wind, and it is the one to open again before a trip.

All four are estimates built on stated assumptions. Below is what each one calculates, where its limits are and a worked example; each tool page explains the method in full under the calculator.

Sun & Shade Simulator

How it calculates. For the 15th of every month the simulator computes the sun's height and compass bearing every two minutes with the NOAA solar position equations, ignoring sun lower than 2° above the horizon. It treats the balcony as a box and follows a straight line from three spots – the railing top, the middle of the floor at pot height and a point 1 ft (30 cm) out from the back wall – towards the sun. A spot counts as sunny when that line is not blocked by the facade, a side wall, the slab above, a solid railing or the building opposite. The sunny steps add up to hours of direct sun per day.

Limits. It is clear-sky geometry: clouds, trees, awnings, plants on the railing and light reflected from white walls are not included, and the building opposite is modelled as one long wall.

Example. An east-facing balcony in New York, 5 ft deep with an 8.5 ft ceiling, side walls and an open railing: on June 15 the railing gets 6.9 hours, the back wall 5.3 hours; in December the railing gets 4.0 and the back wall 1.6 hours. With a solid railing the middle of the floor drops to 2.0 hours in June.

Pot Weight Planner

How it calculates. It works out the volume of each pot from its top and bottom size and its height (round, square or trough), leaves 1 in (2.5 cm) of headspace, and multiplies the mix volume by a wet weight per litre after watering and draining: about 0.6 kg for a lightweight peat or coir mix, 0.75 kg for standard potting mix and up to 1.65 kg for garden soil. The empty pot is estimated from its wall area, a typical wall thickness and the density of the material, unless you enter its weight.

Limits. Real mixes vary by about a fifth either way, and a saturated pot right after rain is heavier than a drained one. The planner adds weights; it cannot tell you what your balcony may carry – only the building owner or a structural engineer can.

Example. A round terracotta pot 16 in across, 12 in at the base and 14 in tall holds 32.4 L (8.6 gal) of mix. In standard potting mix that is 54 lb (24.3 kg) wet; with the estimated 23 lb pot, the total is 76 lb (34.5 kg). The same pot in plastic comes to 57 lb.

Wind Exposure Check

How it calculates. Six answers – floor level, position on the building, surroundings, recess and side walls, railing type and whether the balcony faces the prevailing wind – each give 0 to 4 points (a loggia gives −1). The total sorts the balcony into Sheltered (up to 2), Moderate (up to 4), Exposed (up to 6.5) or Very exposed. The class also sets a wind factor for the watering planner: 1.0, 1.15, 1.3 or 1.5.

Limits. It is a transparent rule of thumb, not a measurement or a wind-load calculation. Local effects such as a gap between two towers can make a balcony windier than its score.

Example. A 7th-floor balcony near the end of the building, mixed surroundings, partly enclosed, glass railing, prevailing wind unknown: 2 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 + 0.5 = 4.5 points, Exposed.

Watering & Holiday Planner

How it calculates. It starts from a reference evaporation rate for the weather you pick (2 mm a day on cool days up to 8 mm in a heatwave), scales it by your sun hours (0.4 plus 0.06 per hour), the wind factor and the pot material, and applies it to the top area of the pot times a canopy factor for small, bushy or large plants. One millimetre over one square metre is one litre. The usable buffer is one tenth of the mix volume; buffer divided by daily use gives the days between checks, and for a trip it adds 20 % to the water needed and subtracts the buffer.

Limits. It does not know your plant's actual thirst, mulch, saucers or rain. Use the result as a starting rhythm and check the mix with a finger or by lifting the pot.

Example. A 12 in (30 cm) plastic pot with a bushy plant, 6 hours of sun on warm days and a Moderate wind class uses about 0.38 L a day; its buffer of about 1.25 L lasts a little over 3 days, so the advice is "check every 2–3 days". For a week away it needs a reservoir of about 2 L.
