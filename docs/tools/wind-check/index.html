Wind & safety

Balcony Wind Exposure Check

This check sorts your balcony into one of four wind classes, from Sheltered to Very exposed, using six things you can see or find out without a wind meter. It is a transparent points score, not a measurement, and it tells you what each class means for your pots, how you fix them and what you plant.

Six questions about your balcony

How to use it

Answer six questions about your balcony: which floor it is on, whether it sits in the middle of the facade, near an end or on a corner, how built-up the surroundings are, whether the balcony is recessed into the building (a loggia), partly enclosed or projecting, what kind of railing it has, and whether it faces the direction the strong winds usually come from where you live. If you do not know the prevailing wind, choose "unknown"; it counts half.

Count floors the US way, with the street-level floor as floor 1. Look at what is around you at your height, not at street level: a 12th-floor balcony above three-story row houses has open surroundings.

How it calculates

Each answer adds points. The total runs from −1 (a low loggia in a dense street) to 11 (a high, projecting corner balcony in the open, facing the prevailing wind).

The six factors and their points
FactorAnswers and points
Floor levelFloors 1–2: 0 · 3–5: 1 · 6–10: 2 · 11–20: 3 · above 20: 4
Position on the buildingMiddle of the facade: 0 · near an end: 1 · corner: 2
SurroundingsDense buildings of similar height: 0 · mixed: 1 · open (water, parks, low buildings): 2
Recess and side wallsLoggia, recessed: −1 · partly enclosed: 0 · projecting: 1
RailingSolid: 0 · glass: 0 · open bars or cables: 1
Faces the prevailing windNo: 0 · unknown: 0.5 · yes: 1

The classes are: Sheltered up to 2 points, Moderate above 2 up to 4, Exposed above 4 up to 6.5, and Very exposed above 6.5.

The reasoning behind the points: wind speed generally increases with height above the ground; air speeds up around the corners and ends of a building; neighbors of similar height break the wind; side walls and a recess give shelter, while a projecting slab catches wind from three sides. An open railing lets wind sweep across the floor where the pots stand, whereas solid and glass panels block it at pot level, which is why they score the same.

Worked examples

A 3rd-floor loggia in the middle of a facade, in a street of similar buildings, with a solid parapet, not facing the prevailing wind: 1 + 0 + 0 − 1 + 0 + 0 = 0 points, Sheltered.

A 7th-floor balcony near the end of the building, mixed surroundings, partly enclosed, glass railing, prevailing wind unknown: 2 + 1 + 1 + 0 + 0 + 0.5 = 4.5 points, Exposed. Finding out the wind direction would move it to 4 (Moderate) or 5 (Exposed).

A 12th-floor corner balcony, projecting, open surroundings, open railing, facing the prevailing wind: 3 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 10 points, Very exposed.

What each class means in practice:

  • Sheltered: most pots and plants are fine. Hanging baskets and tall plants need only normal care. Lightweight pots are no problem.
  • Moderate: plants dry faster on windy days. Avoid very tall, narrow pots; fix railing planters with adjustable clamp brackets that grip the rail without drilling, not ones that just hook over. Large, soft leaves can tear.
  • Exposed: use wide, low, heavy-based pots and group them against the wall. Railing planters belong on the inside of the railing and must be fixed so they cannot lift. Choose small-leaved, flexible plants and ornamental grasses over large-leaved ones; stake or tie anything tall.
  • Very exposed: nothing loose on the railing, no hanging baskets, no lightweight pots that can tip. Keep plants low and against the wall, and expect windburn on tender leaves. Privacy screens and trellises act as sails here; check building rules first.
Anything that falls from an upper floor can injure someone below. On Exposed and Very exposed balconies, secure or bring in loose pots, saucers, cushions and tools before storms.

Wind also dries pots. The watering planner uses the class to add 0 %, 15 %, 30 % or 50 % to a pot's water use. Plant choices for each class are in windy balcony plants, and bracket advice is in railing planters.

Limits

The score is a rough guide. It does not know your local climate, storm season, funneling between particular towers, or gusts that bounce off the building opposite. If plants on your balcony tear or pots move although the class is Moderate, trust what you see and treat it as a class higher.

FAQ

Why does a glass railing score the same as a solid one?

For pots standing on the floor, both block the wind at pot level. Wind still comes over the top, so plants taller than the railing feel it either way.

How do I find the prevailing wind?

Your national weather service or a local airport's wind rose shows which directions strong winds come from. Trees bent in one direction are a free hint.

Does a ceiling above protect from wind?

Somewhat, from above, but not from the side. That is why the score looks at side walls and recess instead.
