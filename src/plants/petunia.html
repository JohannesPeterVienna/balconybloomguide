Petunias give more flowers per square foot than almost any other balcony plant – if they get full sun, steady water and a lot of fertilizer. They are tender perennials grown as annuals: you buy or sow new plants each spring, enjoy them from May until frost and compost them in fall. On a balcony the two things that decide success are the sun hours at the railing and how often you can water.

Which petunia for a balcony?

Modern petunias are complex hybrids. The Missouri Botanical Garden groups them into grandifloras with flowers up to about 4 in (10 cm) wide, multifloras with smaller but more numerous flowers up to about 2 in (5 cm), and cascading or spreading types. For a balcony:

  • Spreading and trailing petunias (sold under series names) are the usual choice for railing boxes and baskets. They grow long, flowering stems that hang over the edge.
  • Multifloras handle rain and wind better than grandifloras and recover faster after a storm.
  • Grandifloras look spectacular but their large petals collapse in rain; Clemson HGIC advises against them for hot, humid summers.

Million bells (Calibrachoa) look like small petunias and are a separate genus; their care is very similar.

Light: how many sun hours?

Petunias need full sun – Clemson HGIC says so plainly, and in balcony terms that means at least 6 hours of direct sun on the leaves. Most balconies with another balcony above have that only at the railing. The Sun & Shade Simulator shows the railing, middle and back-wall hours for every month; on an east-facing balcony the railing often reaches 6 to 7 hours in June but falls below 5 in September, and petunias there fade earlier in the season. A hanging basket hung from the ceiling near the back wall usually gets too little.

Pot size and weight

Petunias have a large leaf area for their root volume, so bigger containers mean fewer emergencies. Plant spreading types about 10 to 12 in (25 to 30 cm) apart in a railing box at least 6 to 7 in deep, three plants in a 14 in basket or one in a 10 to 12 in pot.

The Pot Weight Planner puts a 12 in round pot at about 12.5 L (3.3 gal) of mix, about 21 lb (9.4 kg) wet in standard potting mix. Baskets are the heavy surprise: a bowl-shaped 14 in basket holds roughly 10 to 15 L of mix and, soaked, weighs about 20 to 30 lb (9 to 14 kg) depending on the mix, liner and plants, all of it on one ceiling hook. Use a hook rated for the weight and fixed into structure your building allows, and see the guide to hanging baskets and rail boxes.

Wind, rain and heat

Petunia petals are soft. Strong wind bruises them, and heavy rain leaves the flowers limp for a day. On balconies the Wind Exposure Check rates Exposed or Very exposed, petunias still grow, but choose multiflora or compact spreading types, keep baskets off the railing side that catches the wind, and accept some tattered flowers. In very windy spots, geraniums or ornamental grasses hold up better.

Heat itself is not the problem as long as the pot does not dry out. A petunia that wilts completely once will usually recover; one that wilts every afternoon will stop flowering.

Watering

Petunias in full sun are the thirstiest plants on most balconies. In summer, expect to water railing boxes and baskets daily and small pots twice a day in a heat wave. Water until it drains from the bottom, and water the mix rather than the flowers. Clemson HGIC notes that baskets and boxes can dry out very quickly and may need watering daily or twice a day in hot weather.

The Watering & Holiday Planner shows why: for a 12 in plastic pot with a large, spreading petunia in 8 hours of sun on hot days, it estimates nearly 1 L of water a day and suggests checking daily. For a week away, it calculates a reservoir of around 7 L for that one pot – a self-watering box or a neighbor is the realistic answer.

Feeding and trimming

Petunias are heavy feeders. Mix slow-release fertilizer into the potting mix at planting, then from early summer give a liquid fertilizer for flowering plants every week or two at the label rate. Yellowing leaves with green veins usually point to iron deficiency, which is common when the mix pH rises; a fertilizer with chelated iron helps.

By mid-summer many petunias get long and bare at the base. Cut a third of the longest stems back by about half; they branch and flower again within two to three weeks. Large-flowered types also benefit from removing spent flowers.

Winter by USDA zone

MBG lists petunias as perennial only in USDA zones 10 and 11; everywhere else they are grown as annuals. Overwintering an old petunia indoors is possible from cuttings but rarely worth the window space – vigor and flowering drop, and fresh plants in spring cost little. Spend the effort on geraniums or fuchsias instead.

Common mistakes

  • Too little sun. Below about 5 hours, petunias stretch and flower poorly.
  • Small pots. They dry out twice a day by July.
  • No feeding after June. Flowering stops and leaves yellow.
  • Never trimming. Leggy plants with flowers only at the tips.
  • Heavy baskets on weak hooks. Weigh a basket after watering and size the hook for it.

FAQ

Do petunias need deadheading?

Many modern spreading types are bred to keep flowering without it. Grandifloras look better and flower longer if you remove spent flowers.

Why are my petunia leaves sticky?

Sticky leaves are normal for petunias – the leaves and stems carry glandular hairs. Honeydew from aphids is a different matter; look for small insects under the leaves.

Can I grow petunias on a shady balcony?

Not well. For shade, fuchsias, begonias and impatiens are better choices.
