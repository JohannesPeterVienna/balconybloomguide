A 16 in (40 cm) terracotta pot filled with ordinary potting mix weighs roughly 75 lb (34 kg) after watering, before you add the plant. This planner works out that number for each of your pots, adds them up and gives you a sheet for the people who know your building.

How to use it

For each pot, give the shape (round, square or a long trough), the top size, the bottom size if the pot tapers, and the height; inside measurements are best. Pick the type of mix and the pot material. If you can weigh the empty pot on a bathroom scale, use that weight; it beats any estimate. Add an estimate for the plant if it is large, and the number of identical pots.

Why the wet weight is the number that matters

After watering and draining, potting mix holds a lot of water in its pores; this is called container capacity. Water weighs 8.3 lb per gallon (1 kg per liter), so a large pot can gain tens of pounds between bone dry and freshly watered. A balcony has to carry the heaviest state of every pot, right after watering or rain, so the planner always uses wet weights.

How it calculates

Volume. A round pot is treated as a truncated cone from its top diameter, bottom diameter and height. Square pots and troughs are treated as tapered boxes and calculated with the prismoidal formula. If you leave the bottom size empty, the sides are taken as straight.

Fill level. The planner leaves 1 in (2.5 cm) free below the rim for watering and follows the taper down to that line, so the mix volume is a little less than the pot volume.

Wet mix. The mix volume is multiplied by a typical wet weight per liter: lightweight peat or coir mix with perlite 0.6 kg (about 5.0 lb per gallon), standard potting mix 0.75 kg (6.3 lb per gallon), compost-rich or loam-based mix 0.85 kg (7.1 lb per gallon), gritty cactus mix 1.05 kg (8.8 lb per gallon), and garden soil 1.65 kg (13.8 lb per gallon). Real mixes vary by about 20 % either way.

The pot. If you do not enter the empty weight, the planner estimates it from the wall and base area times a typical wall thickness and the density of the material, for example about 1/2 in (1.2 cm) of terracotta or 1 in (2.5 cm) of concrete.

Total. Wet mix plus pot plus plant, multiplied by the number of identical pots, then summed over your list. Each pot's weight is also divided by the area of its base, which shows how concentrated the load is.

For a worked example, take a round unglazed terracotta pot 16 in (40 cm) across the top, 12 in (30 cm) at the base and 14 in (36 cm) tall. The pot holds 35.6 L (9.4 gal); filled to the line, that is 32.4 L (8.6 gal) of mix. In standard potting mix that weighs 24.3 kg (54 lb) wet. The estimated pot adds 10.2 kg (23 lb), for a total of 34.5 kg (76 lb). The same size in plastic comes to 25.6 kg (57 lb); in terracotta with lightweight mix, 29.7 kg (65 lb). A 32 in (80 cm) plastic railing trough, 8 in (20 cm) wide and 7 in (18 cm) deep, holds 20.5 L (5.4 gal) of mix and weighs about 16.7 kg (37 lb), all of it hanging on the railing; see railing planters before you choose brackets.

Limits and what the planner does not do

All figures are estimates for typical mixes and walls. A pot without drainage holes after heavy rain, or a full saucer, adds more. People, furniture, a grill and snow are not included.

The planner gives no verdict on whether your balcony can carry these weights. Only the building manager, the owner or a structural engineer can answer that, and where heavy pots stand may matter as much as the total. Print the data sheet and give it to them; the background is in how much weight a balcony can hold.

FAQ

Is a dry pot much lighter?

Yes, but planning with dry weights understates the load.

Why is my concrete pot estimate so heavy?

The planner assumes dense walls about 1 in (2.5 cm) thick. If yours is thinner or fiber cement, weigh it empty and use the real figure.
